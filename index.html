03 / READ THE GEOMETRY
01

Read the surface.

A scalar field gives each point a value. Here colour shows surface slope, estimated from neighbouring samples.

02

Choose a direction.

A vector field assigns directions. Our blend rotates a simple planar direction: a visual introduction, not a principal-curvature or stacking solver.

03

Make some space.

A normal offset moves points away from a surface. Our example illustrates the idea; it does not check self-intersection or fabrication clearance.

04 / KEEP GOING

Small experiments.
A much bigger research story.

STACK connects shape, parts, fabrication and logistics. A compact collection can change how parts are stored and transported; building-scale decisions also involve materials, interfaces, machines and people. Our scenes introduce those relationships without estimating loads, nesting efficiency or transport capacity.

Direction fields

Flexible curves

Discrete Elastic Rods ↗

Bergou et al., 2008. A foundational mathematical model for bending and twisting rods. Academic paper; the animation here uses a sine curve.

Elastica design

Bézier curves that are close to elastica ↗

Brander et al., 2018. Read about the relationship between familiar design curves and elastic curves.

Architectural parts

Curved cutting
